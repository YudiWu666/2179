Gender at the Olympics

Near-zero women's events in 1900 to near-parity by Tokyo 2020.

Long-Term Olympic Identity

The Flow of Medals: How Disciplines Rose and Fell

Band width = medals that year. Hover to isolate a sport; drag the strip below to focus on an era.

Swimming dwarfs every other discipline across nearly every era. Cycling Track swells from 2000 onward as velodrome investment paid off. The 1980 Moscow boycott and the 2000 Sydney surge are marked on the chart.

The 1976 to 1984 gap is striking. Australia boycotted the 1980 Moscow Games, then faced the Soviet counter-boycott at Los Angeles 1984, compressing two cycles of medal-winning opportunity. The streamgraph makes this geopolitical dip visible at a glance.
Long-Term Olympic Identity

From Participants to Podiums

Every Summer Games since 1896, unbroken.

As the team size grew, the medal-winning rate held steady at 20–30%, suggesting the program scaled without diluting quality. The blue line is total athletes; the gold line is medal winners.

Sydney 2000 effect: Australia fielded its largest-ever team at the home Games, and the medal-winning rate matched historical averages; home advantage translated into genuine performance gains.

Conclusion

Summary

Fourth in Paris, top-14 in Milano. A sporting nation whose success spans generations, seasons, and disciplines.

53 Summer medals built on Swimming, Cycling Track, and a deep bench across 13 sports. Six Winter medals from just two disciplines, at a 67% gold-strike rate. Among 91 medal-winning nations in Paris, only three finished above Australia.
